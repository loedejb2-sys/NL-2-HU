From the Netherlands to Hungary —
With Almost Nothing.

A practical, no-nonsense guide for Dutch citizens aged 18–21 who want to build a life in Hungary on a tight budget.

🇳🇱 EU Citizen Rights 🇭🇺 OIF Immigration 💰 Low Cost of Living 📅 Updated 2026
Why Hungary?

Hungary is one of the most affordable EU countries for young Dutch people.

As a Dutch citizen, you have the right to live, work, and study in Hungary without a visa. The cost of living is roughly 40–50% lower than in the Netherlands, and Budapest offers a vibrant international community, cheap public transport, and a growing job market for English speakers.

This guide is built for people in your exact situation: 18–21, Dutch, and with very little money. Every section is practical, linked, and updated for 2026.

🚀 First 3 Steps (Do These Now)

  1. Arrive with your Dutch ID card or passport. You can stay up to 90 days without any registration.
  2. Find a cheap bed. Hostels start at $4–10/night; use them while you look for a room.
  3. Register your address within 93 days of arrival at the immigration office (OIF). This gives you a Registration Certificate and an Address Card (Lakcímkártya).

⚠️ The One Thing You MUST Prove

To stay longer than 90 days, Hungarian law requires you to show “sufficient resources” — meaning you have a job, are a student, or have enough money to support yourself. If you have no money and no job/student status, your registration can be refused.

Bottom line: Get a job or enrol in a course before or immediately after arrival. Even a part-time job counts.
Step 1 — Legal

Registration Certificate & Address Card

As an EEA (EU) citizen, you do not need a visa or residence permit. But if you stay longer than 90 days within a 180-day period, you must register.

How to Register

  • 1
    Go to Enter Hungary (online platform)

    Apply electronically at enterhungary.gov.hu — or visit the regional OIF office in person.

  • 2
    Bring your documents

    Valid Dutch ID/passport + proof of Hungarian address (rental contract, landlord’s written consent, or deed).

  • 3
    Declare your first address

    The office forwards your address to the Budapest Government Office, which issues your plastic Lakcímkártya (Address Card) and mails it to you.

  • 4
    Receive your Registration Certificate

    This certificate + Address Card are your key to banking, healthcare, and discounted transport.

Where to Sleep

Housing on a €0 Budget

Housing is the biggest challenge when you arrive with no money. Here are your realistic options, from cheapest to most stable.

🏠 Option 1: Hostels (First 1–2 Weeks)

Budapest has dozens of hostels with dorm beds starting at $4–10 per night. Use them as a base while you job-hunt and find a room.

  • a&o Budapest City — has a communal kitchen; good for long stays.
  • Hostelworld Budapest — search “long-term stay” filter.

🏠 Option 2: Shared Flat / Room

A room in a shared Budapest flat costs roughly €300–550/month including utilities. Outside Budapest (Pécs, Debrecen), shared rooms can be €150–200/month.

  • Alberlet.hu — Hungary’s main rental site
  • Ingatlan.com — large property portal
  • Facebook groups: Budapest Expats, Rooms for Rent Budapest

🏠 Option 3: Student Dormitory

If you enrol in any Hungarian university, you can apply for a dormitory. Fees are as low as HUF 177,750 per semester (≈ €450) for EU citizens, plus a small deposit. Contact your university’s dormitory office directly.

🏠 Option 4: Intergenerational Housing (Free/Cheap)

Budapest runs the “Reliable Room Rental” (Megbízható Szobabérlet) programme, which pairs young people with elderly homeowners who have a spare room. In exchange for light household help and companionship, you get very low or free rent.

How to apply: Contact the Budapest Municipal Housing Office or search for “Megbízható Szobabérlet Budapest” online. The programme includes a legal rental contract and mediation support.
Income

Getting a Job With No Experience

The minimum wage in Hungary (2026) is HUF 322,800/month gross (≈ €830) for unskilled work, and HUF 373,200 for jobs requiring a qualification. Official source: Hungarian State Treasury.

💰 Money Management

  • Open a Hungarian bank account. EU citizens can open a basic payment account with just a passport. Major banks: OTP, Erste, K&H.
  • Get a prepaid SIM. Telekom, Vodafone, or Yettel. Data plans start from a few euros.
  • Tax: You pay 15% flat income tax + 18.5% social contributions. Under-25s are exempt from income tax on earnings up to the median wage. Info: NAV (Hungarian Tax Authority).
Student Status = Legal Stay

Enrolling in a Hungarian Course or University

Student status is one of the easiest ways to satisfy the “sufficient resources” requirement for staying in Hungary longer than 90 days. It also gives you access to dormitories and discounted transport.

Options for Dutch Students

  • Full degree programmes at ELTE, Corvinus, Semmelweis, or the University of Pécs. Many are taught in English.
  • Language courses — short Hungarian courses can provide student status and are often affordable.
  • Erasmus+ — if you are already enrolled at a Dutch institution, an Erasmus exchange to Hungary is a low-risk way to test living there. Info: Erasmus+ official site.

Student Benefits

  • Dormitory accommodation — as low as HUF 177,750/semester (EU rate) + HUF 40,000 deposit.
  • Discounted Budapest transport pass — HUF 3,450/month, but only if you have a Hungarian residential address. Get your Lakcímkártya first. Info: BKK.
  • 50% discount on intercity trains for ages 14–25 with valid ID. Info: MÁV.
Key link: Stipendium Hungaricum — government scholarship for international students. Check eligibility for Dutch applicants.
Staying Healthy

Healthcare as a Dutch EU Citizen

Your Dutch European Health Insurance Card (EHIC) — on the back of your zorgverzekering card — covers medically necessary treatment during a temporary stay in Hungary. Present it at hospitals, clinics, and GP offices. More info: ehic.nl.

English-Speaking Clinics in Budapest

  • FirstMed Centers (District II) — the reference clinic for expats. Consultations €80–150.
  • Buda Health Center (District XII)
  • International Medical Services (IMS) (District XIII)
Low-cost tip: Hungarian public healthcare is free at the point of use if you have a TAJ card. Once you get any job, your employer registers you automatically. Info: neak.gov.hu.
Living Cheaply

Daily Life on a Tight Budget

🍞 Food

  • University canteen meal: €4–6
  • 1 litre milk: €1 · 1 loaf bread: €1–2
  • Free hot meals: Cedek EMIH soup kitchens in Budapest — open to anyone over 18 with a Hungarian social security card.
  • Food bank: Magyar Élelmiszerbank — free food packages through partner organisations.

🚌 Transport

  • Monthly student Budapest pass: HUF 3,450 (requires Hungarian address). Info: BKK.
  • Single bus ticket: €1 from kiosk, €1.25 from driver.
  • Monthly student bus pass in Pécs: €10. Info: City of Pécs.
  • Budapest–Pécs InterCity (student): €7 one-way. Book: MÁV.

📱 Phone & Internet

  • Prepaid SIM from Telekom, Vodafone, or Yettel.
  • eSIM data plans from €1.91/day or €5 for 3GB/15 days.
  • Free Wi-Fi is available in most cafés, libraries, and hostels.

🎉 Entertainment

  • Cinema ticket (student): €3–4.50. Check Cinema City.
  • Theatre ticket (student): €5–9. Check Jegymester.
  • Party entrance: €0–12.50.

If You Run Out of Money Completely

  • Contact the Dutch Embassy in Budapest. They can advise on emergency loans and repatriation.
  • Hungarian social assistance: Municipalities (önkormányzat) may offer “települési támogatás” (municipal aid). Start with Budapest Municipality.
  • Charity food: Cedek EMIH provides free hot meals in Budapest and the countryside. Requirements: over 18 and a valid Hungarian social security card.
  • Food bank: Magyar Élelmiszerbank distributes free food packages through partner organisations.
Realistic advice: Hungary does not have a generous welfare safety net for EU citizens who arrive with no means. The fastest exit from a no-money situation is any job — even part-time delivery or hostel work.
